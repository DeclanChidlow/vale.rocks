I have not played any of the Grand Theft Auto games, nor do I particularly intend to. The closest I've gotten is watching some of Achievement Hunter's now over decade old GTA IV and GTA V videos and a scarce few bits of Regulation Gameplay. GTA VI has released today, thirteen years after GTA V. Even as someone not invested in the series, I can appreciate that this is a major event. It is probably one of the bigger media releases of my life. The budget for the game is estimated to be in excess of a billion US dollars, and it shows.

The technical brilliance on display is astounding. The game is not photorealistic, and I don't think it is trying to be. It is not cartoonish, but it is stylised and possessive of impressive lighting and interactions. The way hair sits on a character's head and moves tangled with other hairs, the way it rests and slides along a person's back as they twist their body, the way it springs and bounces with movement, the way it whips around in the wind -- it is all so lifelike. More than textures or models, lighting makes a game's visuals, and Grand Theft Auto VI is no exception to this rule. Not only is the general lighting beautiful, but also little things like the way light refracts in bottles and the way it is orchestrated to not only illuminate rooms, but also seep them in vibrant hues. One thing that does look bad about the game, however, is the way the player's character often snaps between animations without smoothly transitioning between states.

The pure population of the world with so many complex objects and <abbr title="Non-Playable Character">NPC</abbr>s is technically impressive in of itself. All of the assets are of consistent quality, too. It is a full, complete, comprehensive world to a level of complexity I find hard to comprehend. As I publish this, only a few hours after launch, there are a few minor bugs that people are picking apart and milking for all they're worth, but the game seems like a well-made, polished experience -- something that isn't a given for a AAA title in recent times. I'm so glad they developed the game in their in-house <abbr title="Rockstar Advanced Game Engine">RAGE</abbr> engine rather than using a general-purpose off-the-shelf option.

It is hard to mention it without the peripheral details, however. The game is seeing no physical release beyond a code-in-box. I imagine this is a direct result of PlayStation's impending phase-out of discs. Also, Rockstar's [alleged anti-union behaviour](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PL4kAQGTLhnB7JXi2to0q4b4TpPi-yIL9y) is disgusting and unforgivable. The only reason for an organisation to stifle a union is to get away with doing worse by their employees.
